# Rewritten flight lesson scripts

## 01_taxi

We start parked with the engine running. Learn roll and pitch as two different yoke movements, check steering and brakes, then taxi to the marked stopping area. Keep clear of the runway. Menu pauses; X repeats the current instruction.

Goal: Demonstrate both control axes, taxi slowly, and stop before the runway. This is a control familiarization exercise, not a complete aircraft preflight.

1. **Yoke: turn left and right**: Find the highlighted two-handled yoke in front of you. Hold it with a grip button. Turn it left, then right, then return it to the middle. Keep the airplane stopped.

   Demonstrated: You demonstrated left and right roll input and centered the yoke.

   Next practice: Turn the yoke, rather than pushing it. Move left, then right, then center.

2. **Yoke: push and pull**: Now check pitch, a different movement. Keep holding the yoke. Push it away from you, pull it toward you, then return it to its middle position.

   Demonstrated: You demonstrated forward and aft pitch input and returned to neutral.

   Next practice: Push the yoke away, pull it back, then return to the middle. Do not turn it.

3. **Rudder and steering**: In the airplane these are foot-pedal inputs. Here, move the right thumbstick left, then right, and let it center. The yoke does not steer the nosewheel.

   Demonstrated: You demonstrated both steering directions and centered the input.

   Next practice: Move the right thumbstick sideways in both directions, then let go.

4. **Hold both brakes**: Pull the throttle knob fully back to idle. Squeeze both index triggers and keep the airplane stopped. Each trigger applies its side of the wheel brakes.

   Demonstrated: You held the airplane stopped with both brakes and idle power.

   Next practice: Use both index triggers together. Check that the throttle is fully back.

5. **Taxi to the connector**: Release both brakes. Grip the throttle and move it forward slightly. Follow the taxiway ahead toward the marker at walking pace, about three to six knots. Steer with the right thumbstick.

   Demonstrated: You reached the connector at a controlled taxi speed.

   Next practice: Use less power to slow down. Follow the taxiway to the marker; the yoke does not steer on the ground.

6. **Turn toward the stopping area**: At the connector, slow down and turn right with the right thumbstick toward the next marker. Straighten the nosewheel as the turn finishes. Stay on the taxiway.

   Demonstrated: You completed the turn toward the stopping area slowly.

   Next practice: Slow before turning, use right rudder, then center the stick. Approach the marker slowly.

7. **Stop before the runway**: Pull the throttle back to idle. Squeeze both index triggers smoothly and stop inside the marked area. Keep the nose short of the runway entry; this lesson ends here.

   Demonstrated: You stopped in the marked area without entering the runway.

   Next practice: Close the throttle earlier and brake gently. Stop before the runway, not on it.

Reflection: Which helped you stop accurately: reducing power early or braking late?

## 02_first_flight

Start airborne at seven thousand feet and ninety knots. Use the outside horizon first, with short instrument checks. Then make a shallow left turn and return right. A heading is the direction the nose points; these lesson targets use the highlighted true-heading dial.

Goal: Use small inputs, maintain a safe attitude, and roll out near the target heading. Beginner targets allow modest deviations; correct them smoothly.

1. **Outside: wings level**: You have control. Look through the windshield. Keep the nose steady and the wings level against the distant horizon, using small yoke movements. Avoid chasing every gust.

   Demonstrated: You held a controlled, nearly wings-level attitude.

   Next practice: Look at the distant horizon. Correct the bank gently, then ease the turning input.

2. **Altitude: 7000 feet**: Find the highlighted altimeter: the round dial with several hands and a number window. Aim for seven thousand feet with small pitch and power corrections, then look outside again.

   Demonstrated: You held altitude near seven thousand feet.

   Next practice: Check whether the altitude is rising or falling before correcting. Return toward seven thousand feet gradually.

3. **Airspeed: 90 knots**: Find the round airspeed dial with the colored arcs. Read its needle on the knots scale. Aim for ninety knots using small power adjustments while keeping the nose steady.

   Demonstrated: You held the target airspeed band without a large bank.

   Next practice: Make a small power adjustment, wait for its effect, and cross-check altitude.

4. **Heading: 090**: Find the heading dial with the rotating compass numbers. Read the fixed index at the top: nine means zero nine zero. Hold that direction, then look outside.

   Demonstrated: You held the nose close to heading zero nine zero.

   Next practice: Read the top index, not the side of the dial. Make a small turn toward zero nine zero.

5. **Left turn to 060**: Check the view to your left for traffic. Turn the yoke left to establish about fifteen degrees of bank, then ease the input. Aim for zero six zero, keeping near seven thousand feet.

   Demonstrated: You rolled out near zero six zero with controlled bank and altitude.

   Next practice: Use a shallow left bank. Before zero six zero arrives at the top index, roll the wings level.

6. **Settle on 060**: As zero six zero reaches the top index, level the wings. Hold about seven thousand feet and ninety knots. Keep scanning outside instead of staring at the dial.

   Demonstrated: You settled on zero six zero with altitude and speed under control.

   Next practice: Level the wings first, then make small corrections to altitude and power.

7. **Right turn to 090**: Check right for traffic. Establish a shallow right bank toward zero nine zero. Begin leveling just before that heading reaches the top index. Use gentle rudder if the slip ball moves off center.

   Demonstrated: You rolled out near zero nine zero in a controlled turn.

   Next practice: Reduce the bank as you approach zero nine zero; avoid waiting until you have passed it.

8. **Settle on 090**: Keep the wings level and the nose on zero nine zero. Settle near seven thousand feet and ninety knots with small corrections. Look outside between instrument checks.

   Demonstrated: You returned to steady flight on the original heading.

   Next practice: Set attitude first, adjust power, then check heading and altitude again.

Reflection: Did you return your eyes outside after checking each instrument?

## 03_climb_descent

Start at seven thousand feet. Climb to seventy-three hundred, level off, descend back, then trim. Set attitude and power before trimming. In this simulator the left thumbstick changes trim during flight; it navigates menus only when a menu is open.

Goal: Anticipate each level-off, then stabilize altitude, heading and speed. Trim reduces sustained yoke input; the controllers cannot reproduce real control forces.

1. **Climb toward 7300**: Add climb power and gently raise the nose. Aim for seventy to eighty knots on the airspeed dial and heading zero nine zero. Watch the altimeter approach seventy-three hundred feet.

   Demonstrated: You approached the climb target at a safe climbing speed.

   Next practice: Use pitch to manage climb airspeed and adequate power for the climb. Do not keep raising the nose if speed falls.

2. **Level at 7300**: Start easing the nose toward level as you approach seventy-three hundred feet. Let the airplane accelerate, then reduce to cruise power. Settle near ninety knots and heading zero nine zero.

   Demonstrated: You stabilized near seventy-three hundred feet.

   Next practice: Anticipate the level-off. Lower the nose smoothly, then adjust power as speed builds.

3. **Descend toward 7000**: Reduce power and lower the nose slightly. Aim for roughly three to five hundred feet per minute down on the vertical-speed dial. Keep heading zero nine zero and approach seven thousand feet.

   Demonstrated: You approached seven thousand feet in a controlled descent.

   Next practice: Use a modest descent, keep speed under control, and prepare to level before seven thousand feet.

4. **Level at 7000**: Begin raising the nose toward level before seven thousand feet. Restore cruise power and settle near ninety knots, heading zero nine zero. Allow the vertical-speed needle to settle.

   Demonstrated: You stabilized near seven thousand feet after the descent.

   Next practice: Restore power as you level off. Watch the altitude trend before making another pitch change.

5. **Trim the steady attitude**: Hold the level attitude with the yoke. Tap the left thumbstick up or down a little, then ease your held yoke input. Keep the direction that reduces the correction needed; reverse it if the correction increases.

   Demonstrated: You adjusted trim and maintained steady flight with reduced yoke input.

   Next practice: Settle attitude and speed first. Use a small trim change, then check whether less yoke input is needed.

Reflection: Did you begin leveling before the target altitude, or chase it after passing it?

## 04_takeoff

Begin stopped and aligned on runway zero eight. This prepared checkpoint supplies the engine setup; it is not a full run-up or takeoff checklist. Review the highlighted controls before practice. During the roll, steer with the right thumbstick and watch the airspeed needle.

Goal: Stay on the runway, lift off near fifty-five knots and climb straight ahead at seventy to eighty knots. Real high-altitude takeoff preparation requires the aircraft checklist and performance planning.

1. **Confirm takeoff setup**: Keep stopped on the runway centerline. Check the flap selector and indication: zero to ten degrees for this exercise. The checkpoint has mixture and carb heat set. Identify the throttle and airspeed dial before rolling.

   Demonstrated: You began stopped, aligned and configured for this checkpoint.

   Next practice: Stay stopped and align with the runway. Check the flap indication before adding power.

2. **Accelerate on centerline**: Release both brakes and smoothly move the throttle fully forward. Look along the centerline; steer with the right thumbstick. Briefly check the airspeed needle. Prepare to lift the nose near fifty-five knots.

   Demonstrated: You accelerated on the runway with takeoff power and directional control.

   Next practice: Use full power smoothly and small rudder corrections. Prepare the rotation before speed runs away.

3. **Lift off**: Near fifty-five knots, pull the yoke back gently to raise the nose. Let the airplane fly off, then adjust pitch toward seventy to eighty knots. Avoid a sudden pull.

   Demonstrated: You lifted off in a controlled nose-up attitude.

   Next practice: Raise the nose gradually. If airspeed falls, ease the nose down rather than pulling harder.

4. **Climb straight ahead**: Climb straight ahead at seventy to eighty knots. Keep the wings nearly level and pass six thousand feet on the altimeter. Continue scanning outside.

   Demonstrated: You established the straight-ahead departure climb.

   Next practice: Use a steady climb attitude, adequate power and small rudder corrections. Monitor the airspeed needle.

Reflection: Were you looking along the centerline, with only brief airspeed checks?

## 05_pattern

Begin in the runway zero eight left-hand pattern at sixty-three hundred feet, with the runway on your left. Follow the marked practice route through downwind, base and final. If alignment or speed cannot be recovered safely, go around. These checkpoints do not authorize a real airport arrival.

Goal: Manage configuration and a controlled approach. The local downwind is south of Jay Road; watch for gliders. This simplified route is a practice exercise, not a validated airport procedure.

1. **Downwind: runway on the left**: Keep the runway on your left. Hold about sixty-three hundred feet and eighty-five knots, following the downwind marker. Use heading two seven zero as a reference and remain south of Jay Road.

   Demonstrated: You reached the downwind checkpoint with altitude and speed controlled.

   Next practice: Keep a steady downwind track; use small wind corrections and keep the runway in sight.

2. **Configure and descend**: At the marked abeam point, reduce power. Once below eighty-five knots, move the flap selector to ten degrees and confirm the indication. Begin a gentle descent toward the next marker.

   Demonstrated: You slowed, selected approach flaps and began descending.

   Next practice: Reduce power first. Confirm a safe airspeed before extending flaps, then manage the descent.

3. **Left base**: Turn left toward heading one eight zero and the base marker. Keep a shallow bank and about seventy to seventy-five knots. Check the runway position. If final needs a steep turn, go around.

   Demonstrated: You reached base with a controlled bank and approach speed.

   Next practice: Slow the turn down. Do not steepen it to force the airplane back toward the runway.

4. **Final: stabilize or go around**: Turn left onto final and center the runway in the windshield. Aim for sixty to seventy knots and a steady descent with landing flaps set. If alignment or speed is not stable, apply power and go around.

   Demonstrated: You established a controlled final approach for this practice segment.

   Next practice: Align early, settle the speed and descent, or choose a go-around before running out of height.

5. **Go-around: power and attitude**: Apply full power, check carb heat cold, and set a controlled climb attitude. If full flaps are selected, reduce them to twenty degrees. Keep the runway direction.

   Demonstrated: You applied go-around power and kept a safe flap transition.

   Next practice: Power and safe pitch come first. Do not retract all the flaps at once.

6. **Go-around: positive climb**: Confirm a positive climb on the vertical-speed dial and at least sixty knots. Reduce flaps to ten degrees while keeping the runway direction.

   Demonstrated: You maintained a positive climb while reducing flaps to ten degrees.

   Next practice: Wait for a positive climb and safe speed before the next flap change.

7. **Go-around: clean climb**: Once clear of obstacles, retract the remaining flaps and continue climbing straight ahead at seventy to eighty knots.

   Demonstrated: You completed the go-around with a clean, controlled climb.

   Next practice: Retract the final flap stage only when safely climbing and clear of obstacles.

Reflection: Would a go-around have reduced your workload compared with trying to rescue the approach?

## 06_landing

Begin already established on a practice final, with twenty degrees of flaps. Stabilize, land and slow down, or choose a go-around. This is not a straight-in arrival procedure for Boulder. Review the throttle, airspeed and flap references before practice.

Goal: A controlled landing or a controlled go-around is a valid outcome. Do not force an unstable approach onto the runway. The exercise observes aircraft state, not every real-world checklist action.

1. **Final: speed and alignment**: Keep the runway centered ahead. Aim for sixty to seventy knots on the airspeed dial and a steady descent with the wings nearly level. If you cannot stabilize, go around.

   Demonstrated: You established stable alignment, speed and descent before the landing.

   Next practice: Make small corrections early. If the approach remains unstable, apply power and go around.

2. **Round out and touch down**: As you approach the runway, shift your view farther along it. Reduce power and gradually raise the nose to ease the descent. Let the main wheels settle; do not push the airplane onto the runway.

   Demonstrated: You touched down within this exercise's runway and attitude limits.

   Next practice: Look farther down the runway, reduce power smoothly, and ease the descent. Go around if the landing cannot be made safely.

3. **Maintain control on rollout**: Keep the nose tracking along the centerline with gentle rudder. Let the nosewheel lower, hold idle power and brake smoothly with both index triggers to a slow taxi pace.

   Demonstrated: You slowed on the runway with directional control.

   Next practice: Keep steering as you slow. Apply both brakes smoothly; do not let the airplane drift off the pavement.

4. **Go-around: power and attitude**: Apply full power, check carb heat cold, and set a controlled climb attitude. If full flaps are selected, reduce to twenty degrees. Keep the runway direction.

   Demonstrated: You began a controlled go-around rather than forcing a landing.

   Next practice: Use full power and safe pitch. Keep some flap until the climb is established.

5. **Go-around: positive climb**: With a positive climb and at least sixty knots, reduce flaps to ten degrees. Keep the wings nearly level and continue along the runway direction.

   Demonstrated: You kept a positive climb through the next flap reduction.

   Next practice: Check climb and airspeed before reducing flaps further.

6. **Go-around: clean climb**: Clear of obstacles, retract the remaining flaps. Continue a straight-ahead climb at seventy to eighty knots.

   Demonstrated: You completed a clean go-around climb.

   Next practice: Wait until safely climbing clear of obstacles, then retract the final flap stage.

Reflection: Did you decide early enough whether to continue landing or go around?

## Cockpit reference explanations

**Outside**: Look through the windshield at the distant horizon, where sky meets terrain. Use it to judge pitch and bank; briefly cross-check the instruments, then look outside for traffic again.

**Attitude**: Find the round blue-and-brown instrument in the upper center of the main gauge group. Its fixed miniature airplane is your reference; the artificial horizon moves behind it. Compare it with the outside horizon.

**Airspeed**: Find the round dial with colored arcs, left of the blue-and-brown attitude instrument. Its needle reads speed through the air in knots, not taxi speed over the ground. Use the target in the current instruction.

**Altimeter**: Find the round multi-hand dial to the right of the attitude instrument. Its number window and hands show altitude in feet above sea level. Read the whole altitude, not only the fast-moving hundreds hand.

**Heading**: Find the rotating compass-card dial below the attitude instrument. Read the fixed index at the top; 9 means 090 degrees. This simulator uses true heading here, so use this highlighted dial for lesson targets.

**VerticalSpeed**: Find the round vertical-speed dial below the altimeter. Zero means level; the needle shows climb or descent in feet per minute. It lags attitude changes, so wait for the trend instead of chasing the needle.

**Slip**: Find the small ball in the curved tube at the bottom of the turn instrument. Center it with gentle rudder toward the displaced ball. The right thumbstick supplies rudder input in this simulator.

**Throttle**: Find the highlighted throttle knob below the instrument panel. Hold a grip over it and move it forward for more power, back for less. Identify this knob by its highlight before moving it; do not confuse it with mixture.

**Yoke**: The two-handled control in front of your seat is the yoke. Turning it left or right changes bank. Pushing it away or pulling it toward you changes pitch. Hold a controller grip to move it. These are two different motions.

**Flaps**: Find the highlighted flap selector beside the center controls. Move it to the required detent and confirm the flap indication. For these lessons, slow below eighty-five knots before extending flaps. Use each indicated position, not an estimated lever movement.

**Trim**: Trim reduces the yoke input needed to hold an established attitude. During flight, tap the left thumbstick up or down slightly and ease the held yoke input. Reverse the trim direction if the correction grows. Set attitude and power first; there is no force feedback.

**Runway**: Look outside for the paved runway, its centerline and edges. The elevated marker identifies the current practice area. Taxi only on the taxiway and stop before the runway entry; the marker is a teaching aid, not a clearance.
