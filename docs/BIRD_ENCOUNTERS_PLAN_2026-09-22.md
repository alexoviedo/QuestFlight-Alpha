## Recommendation

Keep the accepted animated eagle, repair its encounter/session integration, and add a small shared population of red-tailed hawks, ospreys and Canada geese at authored habitat sites. Give Demo one deliberately composed eagle encounter with Michael's brief, conditional callout. Free Flight and every training lesson use the same habitat birds without compulsory close passes. Start with these four recognizable silhouettes, not a large generic flock system or a full ecosystem simulation.

Success means a bird is convincingly visible from the normal pilot seat, moves like its species, remains continuous when the pilot looks away and back, and fits the measured Quest budget. An enabled GameObject, a close external beauty shot or a successful route trigger is not visual success.

## Verified diagnosis and limits

Inspected `GoldenEagleThermalSoaring.cs`, its prefab/model/controller/source manifest, nested environment/world references, `DemoTourAuthoring.cs`, the active wind profile, mode coordination, narration, and the existing September 21 native route traces. No new Unity run was needed to establish these source/data findings.

1. **The eagle was not removed.** The product still contains one enabled eagle with its aircraft observer and demo-pilot references. The candidate Android build report includes its model, materials, textures and controller. Its implementation and wildlife assets have no diff from the existing branch baseline. There is one 3,858-triangle skinned renderer, one material, 1024-square texture set and five authored animation states. The runtime controller uses AlwaysAnimate; there is no new bird distance/LOD setting to blame.
2. **The encounter is still coupled to old route geometry.** The actual prefab requires segment index >=1, a horizontal distance <=1,250 m from ENU (1700, 2600), and a negative dot product against the fixed old approach direction (-0.3162, 0.9487). The new scenic route and wind-dependent runway selection were added without adapting this contract.
3. **Runway 26 definitively misses that trigger in the retained full native trace.** Its nearest sampled approach to the thermal is approximately 1,359 m; no saved sample satisfies the encounter envelope. The ordinary runway-08 trace does enter: about 110 seconds, segment 1, approximately 1,220 m from the thermal. Therefore, saying that the new route universally disables the trigger would be incorrect. The shipped wind currently favors 08; the 26 failure alone does not establish the cause of the user's particular missed sighting.
4. **The runway-08 encounter is easy to miss.** An approximate reconstruction of the existing cubic path against the saved 1 Hz aircraft trace starts the bird 122 m away, puts it about 41 m away after four seconds, approximately abeam after six seconds, and behind after eight. This uses horizontal ground track as a heading proxy and an approximate origin elevation; it is not a tracked-headset replay. It establishes a short visibility opportunity, not the exact view the user saw. There is no check that the pilot can see the bird before the encounter is consumed.
5. **Replay and mode behavior are defective.** `_encounterStaged` is never reset by a new demo, restart or mode change. Once used, the one-shot stays used for the lifetime of the scene instance. Free Flight and training stop `ProductDemoPilot`; with its reference still non-null, neither the route gate nor the null-reference proximity fallback can trigger. The existing ambient orbit still runs in those modes; birds are not globally disabled, but there is only one remote bird to chance upon.
6. **Current soaring is not meaningfully driven by lift.** The eagle maintains a fixed 55 m circle at Unity height 578 m. Wind changes facing/flapping, not its altitude or orbit center. The newer shared wind field displaces thermal centers downwind with height. With the authored -5.5 m/s upper wind, the legacy thermal's center at that height shifts roughly 495 m west; the bird continues circling the old ground center. This breaks the intended visible-lift cue. It is a separate behavior flaw, not proof of disappearance.
7. **The new narration cannot point out the bird as currently authored.** There is no eagle cue. Landmark cues use fixed positions and a minimum distance of 300 m; blindly adding an eagle as another landmark would exclude the existing close pass. A moving-subject cue needs its own eligibility rules.

There is no retained headset trace identifying the runway, encounter latch, pilot gaze and bird position during the reported run. The findings above establish concrete integration defects and a visibility weakness; they do not invent a single proven event history for that run.

## Exact behavior plan

### Shared wildlife, independent of demo progression

- Author habitat volumes and a bounded set of bird actors in the existing environment prefab using Editor tooling. Serialize references to the native aircraft, pilot camera, terrain, water and shared wind. No runtime construction/discovery of the product hierarchy.
- Initial total roster ceiling: **24 birds**: one eagle, three hawks, two ospreys, and three groups of six geese. This is an implementation cap, not an ecological census or a target to keep constantly visible. Distribute habitats across the actual covered map; avoid concentrating all groups near the airport.
- A lightweight director controls habitat eligibility, travel/circle/avoidance states and visibility priority. Birds remain in world space, with stable session identities and deterministic variation. Free Flight and training use chance encounters along their natural routes; no relocation to chase the pilot.
- A new flight selection resets session state under the existing transition mask. Pause freezes wildlife with flight; resume restores continuous motion. Training checkpoint resets may re-establish nearby habitat activity under the existing fade, never teleport a visible bird. Demo-to-manual handoff preserves all existing bird positions.
- Both player and ambient aircraft influence a short-horizon closest-approach avoidance calculation. Apply smooth, bounded heading/altitude changes early. Keep planned trajectories out of runways, buildings, terrain, trees and aircraft volumes; do not rely solely on emergency avoidance. No bird strikes or damage changes in this work package.

### Demo eagle encounter

1. Author one encounter corridor for each supported departure direction on a low-workload, settled portion of the actual scenic climb/cruise route. Bind to an explicit route event/segment identity and spatial conditions, not a raw segment index plus an unrelated thermal radius.
2. Preposition the same eagle along a plausible world-space ingress while it is outside the pilot's visible region; do not pop it into the windshield. Once visible, never relocate it to maintain the encounter. If no valid preparation window exists, defer the event to the next authored opportunity.
3. Compose a visible forward/forward-side pass that does not require looking directly below, behind a pillar or above the roof. Start tuning around a 200–300 m acquisition range and a **50–100 m planned lateral offset**, with a clear aircraft-separation corridor. Retain realistic eagle size and approximately the existing 13–15 m/s flight speed as initial tuning values. Do not accelerate the eagle to pace the C172 or slow the aircraft just for a bird shot.
4. Aim for a **6–10 second useful pilot-view window**, validated from actual recorded motion and ordinary pilot eye positions. If those targets conflict at the actual closure speed, adjust ingress direction/timing, not bird scale or a camera-relative constraint. The passing bird must remain legible at accepted render resolution/FFR.
5. Add short Michael recordings such as “Golden eagle ahead, slightly left.” Select left/right/above wording from current aircraft-relative bearing. Require a genuinely visible, sufficiently large moving subject, clear cockpit/terrain sightline and enough predicted remaining viewing time. Do not announce a bird hidden by the cockpit or already behind the pilot. Facts about riding lift require sampled positive lift; otherwise use a neutral species observation.
6. After the pass, follow a continuous, speed-limited route to the nearest suitable authored open-country/foothill habitat. Use the shared field's wind-displaced lift center and terrain-aware altitude envelope. It may take several minutes to reach distant foothills; do not rush that journey to fit a narration sentence. The eagle remains available for later chance sightings.
7. If the user takes over or diverts, stop the scripted encounter scheduling and retain natural bird motion/avoidance. Discard stale queued speech, but finish a sentence already being spoken when the user looks away. Training/safety/status interruptions use a brief audio fade and retain priority. Returning control does not replay a completed encounter; a genuinely unstarted encounter can be attempted once at a later valid location. Restarting a new demo rearms it.

### Convincing flight and animation

- Separate airspeed and wind advection. Integrate ground motion from the bird's air-relative movement plus shared wind; do not claim constant airspeed while only crabbing a fixed-speed ground path.
- Use bounded visual flight kinematics, not another flight-dynamics backend: smooth acceleration, continuous tangents, turn-rate-related bank, gradual pitch and tail adjustments. Scale orbit radius with speed/bank rather than use an arbitrary circle and unrelated bank angle.
- Raptors alternate real articulated wingbeats and subtle gliding motion. Climb where updraft exceeds the species' configured sink tendency; descend or flap when lift weakens; leave the thermal near its cap and glide to another habitat. Ridge soaring is restricted to windward terrain where the shared sampler supplies usable lift. Do not revise aircraft weather/physics for this feature.
- Geese keep realistic body/neck posture and offset wingbeat phases. One lightweight group path plus individual spacing/avoidance is sufficient; no expensive all-pairs flock simulation. Turns loosen the V instead of rotating an entire rigid formation.
- Keep feet tucked in flight, primary-feather silhouettes distinct, tails responsive to turns, and upper/lower wing coloration appropriate. Inspect against sky and terrain at actual cockpit distances. Fine feather geometry invisible at those distances is not worth its cost.
- V1 does not require prey actors, kills, nests, chicks, close-ground walking or an osprey splash/catch cycle. Only add a landing/perching/dive behavior if its contact geometry and animation can be convincingly supported by the admitted assets. Searching/hunting flight is enough to establish ecology without an unfinished close-up behavior.

## Quest 3 budget and LOD

These are proposed starting ceilings, **not measured headroom or performance promises**. The previous scene is already demanding; allocate by visibility and prove the added cost on Quest without lowering accepted scenery distance, resolution or Medium FFR.

| Resource | Initial ceiling / policy |
| --- | --- |
| Total logical birds | 24 across authored habitats; offscreen state remains cheap and continuous. |
| Visible birds | At most 16; at most four close full-detail birds. Prioritize the demo eagle and existing visible actors; keep flock groups coherent. Suppress new distant groups before dropping nearby visible birds. |
| Close geometry | Approximately 3.4–4.1k triangles per bird, one renderer/material where practical. Eagle remains at existing detail. |
| Middle/distant geometry | Offline silhouette-preserving reductions: roughly 600–1,000 triangles, then 150–300 for small silhouettes. Do not replace prominent birds with flat cards. |
| Total visible bird geometry | Target <=30k triangles and <=16 additional draw submissions before measured optimization. Do not assume skinned meshes automatically instance. |
| Textures/lighting | Shared per-species 512–1024 textures, Android ASTC, existing simple lit path, no new shadow maps, reflection probes, lights or feather-layer transparency stacks. Target <=8 MB additional bird textures/meshes/clips after import. |
| CPU | Stagger habitat/wind/avoidance decisions around 5–10 Hz, interpolate visual movement every frame; no per-frame allocation or per-bird physics bodies. Near collision prediction uses the existing aircraft velocity and a bounded actor list. |
| Performance acceptance | Provisional incremental targets: <=0.3 ms CPU and <=0.4 ms GPU at the representative worst bird view, assessed with matched conditions and variability. Also check sustained frame cadence; do not claim 72 Hz from these limits. If exceeded, reduce concurrent distant birds/animation work before degrading the close encounter. |

LOD/culling details:

- Choose LOD using projected wingspan/angular size in the actual stereo view, with distance bounds and hysteresis. A real 2.1 m wingspan is approximately 1 degree at 120 m and only 0.4 degrees at 300 m; correct distance and contrast matter more than elaborate distant feathers. Pixel cutoffs must be tuned under Medium FFR, not from desktop resolution alone.
- Use conservative bounds covering the complete animated wingspan and the union of both eyes, with a head-turn margin. Never key visibility only to aircraft forward or use a permanent rear/under-aircraft exclusion: banking and head turns change what the pilot can see.
- Frustum-cull invisible renderers. Retain the full-detail hero's accepted AlwaysAnimate behavior; the prior project explicitly rejected frozen poses caused by naive animation culling. For distant/offscreen birds, preserve phase/time and world trajectory, and evaluate the current pose before re-enabling rendering. Do not use a culling flag without that recovery contract. [Unity skinned-renderer bounds guidance](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-SkinnedMeshRenderer.html).
- Do not use instantaneous camera visibility to despawn/reseed birds. Cull subpixel representations consistently in both eyes; do not fade a clearly visible nearby flock out to satisfy the count cap. Admit populations conservatively so normal views fit the cap.
- Avoid introducing VAT/GPU flocking/complex occlusion infrastructure up front. With this population, mesh/texture reuse, restrained animation and ordinary stereo-safe culling are the fastest credible first implementation. Measure before adding another rendering system.
